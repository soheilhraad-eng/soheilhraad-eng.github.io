I have talked about the emotional journey of belonging before. But sometimes, the barrier
isn't just emotional. It is structural.

I stood at a bank counter recently, documents in hand, only to be told I couldn't open an
account. It wasn't because of my credit history. It was simply because of a line on my
documents.

In that moment, rules written far away from that counter stopped being abstract. Once again,
they became a wall between me and my ability to exist.

To the person behind the glass, I was no longer a professional or a nice guy. I was simply a
risk category.

I have found that this is one of the specific, silent struggles of migration for people like
me that we rarely discuss: the exhaustion of being physically present but structurally
excluded.

{% include slides.html %}

This experience (and those like it) triggers a profound conflict in my sense of identity. It
creates a split between who I know I am and who the system says I am.

The solution isn't just to "adapt" or "think positively." It requires me to build a new layer
of self, one capable of holding my dignity intact while navigating a system designed to keep
me out.

**How can you maintain your identity when the world labels you as a liability?**
